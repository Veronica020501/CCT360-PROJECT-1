User Experience and Accessibility within Makerspaces

This project examined the user experience and accessibility of the Square One Makerspace and Hazel McCallion Library. The research was divided into four key components:

  1. Context and Inquiry, which outlined the primary issues identified
  2. Workflow, which used two models to visually represent the systems and processes involved
  3. Participatory Findings, which gathered observations from participants exploring the spaces
  4. Final Findings, which consolidated the research into key insights and opportunities for improving the overall user experience and accessibility.

Project Challenge:

Users experienced hesitation and uncertainty when navigating the Hazel McCallion Library Makerspace due to limited instructions, insufficient resources, and fear of damaging unfamiliar equipment.

Solution:

Conducted contextual research, workflow modeling, and participatory design sessions with four participants to identify accessibility barriers, user frustrations, and opportunities for improvement.

Results / Key Outcomes:

  1. Identified three key themes: low user confidence, limited assistance and instruction, and the need for upgraded support systems
  2. Recommended clearer instructional materials, increased staff support, beginner-friendly workshops, and improved access to resources.
  3. Developed design implications to create a more welcoming, accessible, and user-friendly makerspace.
